3. EFFICIENT MARKET HYPOTHESIS - CONCLUSION
========================================================================================================

Across 98 NSE stocks, a linear model given 7 years of
daily prices and four of the most widely known technical inputs - two
moving averages, their ratio, and the day's own return - predicted the
direction of tomorrow's move 49.6% of the time, against a naive
always-up baseline of 47.8%. The average edge was +1.85%, and
68 of 98 stocks (69%) finished above their own baseline.

Read the accuracy figure on its own first: 49.6% is below a coin
flip. The positive average edge is not skill, it is a weak benchmark.
Over the test window (2025-02-01 to 2026-06-26) the always-up rule only
scored 47.8%, because the window was close to flat: the median
stock moved -0.1% and 49 of 98 rose at all.
Beating a poor benchmark from below a coin flip is not a result worth
acting on.

Average R2 on returns was -0.0155. Note the sign: on average the
model predicted tomorrow's return slightly WORSE than simply guessing the
mean return every day.

Test periods average 345 days, so the standard error on a
single stock's directional accuracy is roughly 2.7%. Two standard
errors is about 5%, so any one stock's edge inside that band is
indistinguishable from luck - and with 98 stocks ranked, a
handful will clear it by chance alone. The top of the ranked table should
be read as sampling variation, not discovered signal, until it survives
an untouched second period.

This is the weak form of the efficient market hypothesis behaving exactly
as advertised: information already contained in the public price history
is already reflected in the price. Moving averages and lagged returns are
the most heavily studied indicators in existence; any edge they carried
would have been arbitraged away long before it reached a 7-year
sample of large, mid and small-cap NSE names.

The golden cross specifically - tested here as ma_ratio - was the largest
weight for 0 of 98 stocks, and its average absolute
weight was 0.00162, close enough to zero that the model is
effectively ignoring it. Its sign split 61 positive to 37
negative, so 62% of fits read golden-cross
territory as nudging tomorrow higher and the rest read it the other
way. A signal carrying real directional information would point the same
way for nearly every stock and would earn a weight large enough to act
on. This one does neither.

Honest caveats. This tests linear relationships only, at a one-day
horizon, on price-derived features alone; none of that rules out
non-linear structure, longer horizons, or signals in volume,
fundamentals, order flow or news. Dividends are excluded and transaction
costs are ignored, both of which flatter the momentum side. Two
uncorrected demergers (Raymond, Tata Motors) sit inside the test period.
And 7 years is short for a question the literature answers over
decades.

The finding is not that markets are unbeatable. It is the narrower and
better-supported claim that these particular features, at this horizon,
carry no exploitable information for this universe.
