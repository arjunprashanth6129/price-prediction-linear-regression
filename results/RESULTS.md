## 3. Feature importance

Which feature carried the single largest absolute coefficient, per stock:

| Feature | Largest weight for | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| `ma5` | 58 | 59% |
| `ma20` | 38 | 39% |
| `ret` | 2 | 2% |
| **`ma_ratio`** | **0** | **0%** |

The golden cross ratio was never the most heavily weighted feature for any of the
98 stocks.

### `ma_ratio` in detail

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Mean absolute coefficient | **0.00162** |
| Positive coefficient | 61 stocks (62%) |
| Negative coefficient | 37 stocks (38%) |
| Largest weight for | 0 stocks |

A signal carrying real directional information would point the same way for
nearly every stock and would earn a coefficient large enough to move a
prediction. This one splits close to 60/40 and sits three decimal places from
zero.

**Caveat.** `ma_ratio` is `ma5 / ma20`, a deterministic function of two features
already in the model. Collinear predictors let a linear fit trade offsetting
weights between them without changing any prediction, so individual coefficients
are unstable and this table is weaker evidence than it appears. The directional
accuracy in section 2 does not depend on it.

Two things to note before reading the top of that table as a shortlist.

First, four of the top five have **negative R²**: they called direction right
slightly more often than their baseline while modelling the *size* of the move
worse than a constant guess would. That combination is what noise looks like, not
what signal looks like.

Second, with ±5.38 points of noise on any single figure, only 16 of 98 stocks
land outside the band, which is about what ranking 98 noisy estimates produces by
chance. Nothing in this table should be treated as discovered until it survives
an untouched second period.

## 6. The worked example

Reliance. Two models, identical features, identical split. Only the target
differs. The third column is the rule that requires no model at all.

Trained 2019-07-01 to 2025-01-31; tested on 347 days, 2025-02-01 to 2026-06-26.

| Test-set score | Price model | Naive: tomorrow = today | Return model |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| MAE | ₹18.23 | **₹13.26** | 0.0097 |
| RMSE | ₹23.70 | **₹18.08** | 0.0133 |
| R² | 0.9363 | **0.9629** | -0.0080 |
| Directional accuracy | 46.97% | 51.59% | 51.01% |

The price model's R² of 0.9363 is beaten by doing nothing (0.9629), which also
has 27% less average error and better directional accuracy. A price-level R² near
0.94 measures autocorrelation, not forecasting skill.

MAE and RMSE in the first two columns are rupees and directly comparable. The
return model predicts a fraction, so only its R² and direction can be read
across.

### Coefficients

| Feature | Price model (₹ per SD) | Return model (fraction per SD) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `ma5` | +277.4266 | -0.0188 |
| `ma20` | -18.1000 | +0.0178 |
| `ret` | +11.6955 | -0.0012 |
| `ma_ratio` | +1.8851 | +0.0025 |
| bias | +1054.5439 | +0.0008 |

In the return model, `ma5` at -0.0188 and `ma20` at +0.0178 are near-equal and
opposite: two collinear features cancelling out, which is the coefficient-level
statement of "these inputs carry nothing".

### Prediction ranges over the test period

| | Range |
|---|---|
| Actual next-day return | -4.60% to +5.26% |
| Predicted next-day return | within ±0.47% of zero |
| Mean absolute daily move | 0.97% (≈ ₹13 on a mean close of ₹1,387) |

The predictions occupy about a tenth of the range of what actually happened. When
inputs carry no signal, least squares converges on the training mean, and the
mean daily move of a large stock is approximately zero. The flat line is the
correct answer to the question, not a broken model.

## 8. Conclusion

Across 98 NSE stocks over seven years, a linear model given four of the most
widely followed technical indicators predicted the direction of tomorrow's move
49.63% of the time, below a coin flip, with an average R² of -0.0155 on returns.
The golden cross ratio was never the model's heaviest feature and carried an
average absolute weight of 0.0016. Only 16 of 98 per-stock results fall outside
the band where chance alone explains them.

This is the weak form of the efficient market hypothesis behaving as advertised.
Information already in the public price history is already in the price.

The finding is not that markets are unbeatable. It is the narrower and
better-supported claim that **these particular features, at this horizon, carry
no exploitable information for this universe.**

Limitations that bear on how far this generalises are listed in
[the README](../README.md#7-limitations).
