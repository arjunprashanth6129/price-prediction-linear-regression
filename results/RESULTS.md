over this benchmark, which exceeds the model's +1.85 points.

### `ma_ratio` in detail

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Mean absolute coefficient | **0.00162** |
| Positive coefficient | 61 stocks (62%) |
| Negative coefficient | 37 stocks (38%) |
| Largest weight for | 0 stocks |

A sign test of 61 positive against 37 negative gives p = 0.02, but the mean
absolute coefficient is 0.0016 (0.16 percentage points of daily return per
standard deviation), and the out-of-sample accuracy in section 2 shows no
benefit from it.

`ma_ratio` is a deterministic function of `ma5` and `ma20`, which are themselves
highly correlated. Under this multicollinearity, individual coefficients have
high variance, so this table is weaker evidence than the accuracy results in
section 2, which do not depend on how the fit is divided among the regressors.

Four of the five largest edges occur with a negative R²: those models predicted
the sign of the return slightly more often than the benchmark while predicting
its magnitude less accurately than a constant.

Sixteen stocks lie outside the band of ±5.38 points, 15 above it and one below.
This count should not be read as 16 discoveries. The band is centred on zero,
while the edges are centred on +1.85 points because of the weak benchmark, so
the distribution is shifted towards the upper boundary. Tested directly against
50%, no stock's accuracy is significantly above chance.

## 6. The worked example

Reliance Industries. Two regressions with identical features and split; only the
target differs. The middle column is the random-walk forecast, which sets the
next close equal to the current close and requires no estimation.

Trained 2019-07-01 to 2025-01-31; tested on 347 days, 2025-02-01 to 2026-06-26.

| Test-set score | Price model | Random walk | Return model |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| MAE | ₹18.23 | **₹13.26** | 0.0097 |
| RMSE | ₹23.70 | **₹18.08** | 0.0133 |
| R² | 0.9363 | **0.9629** | -0.0080 |
| Directional accuracy | 46.97% | 51.59% | 51.01% |

The random walk has a higher R² than the price model (0.9629 against 0.9363),
27% lower mean absolute error and higher directional accuracy. The price model's
high R² reflects the autocorrelation of price levels, not forecasting ability.

MAE and RMSE in the first two columns are in rupees and comparable with each
other. The return model's errors are fractions of the price.

### Coefficients

| Feature | Price model (₹ per SD) | Return model (fraction per SD) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `ma5` | +277.4266 | -0.0188 |
| `ma20` | -18.1000 | +0.0178 |
| `ret` | +11.6955 | -0.0012 |
| `ma_ratio` | +1.8851 | +0.0025 |
| bias | +1054.5439 | +0.0008 |

In the return model, the coefficients on `ma5` (-0.0188) and `ma20` (+0.0178)
are nearly equal and opposite, the pattern produced when two correlated
regressors are fitted to noise.

### Prediction ranges over the test period

| | Range |
|---|---|
| Actual next-day return | -4.60% to +5.26% |
| Predicted next-day return | within ±0.47% of zero |
| Mean absolute daily move | 0.97% (≈ ₹13 on a mean close of ₹1,387) |

The forecasts span about a tenth of the range of realised returns. When the
regressors are uninformative, the least squares forecast converges to the
training mean of the target, which for a large stock's daily return is close to
zero.

## 8. Conclusion

Across 98 NSE stocks, a linear regression on four moving-average features
predicted the sign of the next day's return 49.63% of the time, with a mean
out-of-sample R² of -0.0155. No stock's accuracy is significantly above 50%, and
the edge over the always-up benchmark is smaller than that of a random forecast.
`ma_ratio` is the largest coefficient for no stock. The results are consistent
with weak-form market efficiency for these features, a linear model and a
one-day horizon.

Limitations are listed in [section 7 of the report](../docs/REPORT.md#7-limitations).
